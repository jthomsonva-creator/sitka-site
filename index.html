Inside the app

A tuner, a songbook, a vocal trainer and a metronome

Four tabs, and they know about each other — the tuning you pick in one is the tuning the others work in.

  • The chord picker inside the song editor. Every chord in your key with a line on what it does to a progression, and the playable fingerings underneath, best first. Tap one to drop it into the line you are writing.

  • The tuner reading the first string, mid-pluck. The trace is the last few seconds of pitch, so you watch a string settle rather than guess. Pick a different tuning from the row below and the readout, the string buttons and the chord shapes all change with it.

  • The full library, grouped by family. Each tuning carries a line on why you would reach for it — Orkney is stacked fifths and endless drone, All Fourths drops the B-string exception — so it reads as a menu rather than a list of names.

  • Words with the chords sitting above them where you put them, a syllable count on every line, and sections you can add and reorder. It is not a private file format: export as ChordPro or plain text whenever you like.

  • Sing a note and it measures three things separately — how much of the time you were in tune, whether you sat sharp or flat, and how long you took to find the note. The wave is vibrato rather than error, which is why the verdict comes from a one-second average.

  • Eight time signatures, four ways to divide the beat, and tap-to-set tempo. The pendulum swings in time, and it stops itself when the tuner or the vocal trainer needs the microphone.

What "ranked" means

Three ways to play G. They are not equal.

Every chart gives you a shape. Sitka gives you the ones worth learning first, and says why: open strings ring longer than stopped ones, the root in the bass sounds like the chord you asked for, and fewer fingers means you can change to it in time.

Five points between first and third — one open string, and one finger.

The tuner

Built to be right, not to look busy

Pitch detection on the YIN algorithm, verified against reference tones. It reads a low E as a low E instead of flipping an octave, stays quiet between plucks in a noisy room, and reads the sample rate from your phone's hardware rather than assuming it — the shortcut that leaves some tuners consistently sharp on some phones.

Play the guitar and it will capture the tuning you have already found by ear, string by string.

Songwriting

Somewhere to put the song

A writing side that knows what key you are in: diatonic chords with roman numerals, borrowed chords when you want the surprise, and a rhyme dictionary that ranks by sound rather than spelling. Chords sit above the words, where you put them.

Export as ChordPro or plain text, whenever you like.

Also inside

A metronome, and a trainer for your ear

A metronome with meters and subdivisions. And a vocal trainer that reports three numbers rather than one — how long you held the note, whether you sat sharp or flat, and how long you took to find it — because those are three different problems with three different fixes.

It builds every exercise inside the range you gave it and never above it.

Privacy

It stays on your phone

This is not a promise about our intentions. It is a property of how the app is built.

  • No account, and nothing to sign up for.
  • No analytics, no ads, no tracking of any kind.
  • The only thing Sitka connects to is Google Play, to verify an in-app purchase. There is no server of ours for it to reach, because we do not run one.
  • The microphone is used to measure pitch. Audio is analysed as it arrives and discarded; nothing is recorded and nothing is stored.
  • Your songs, tunings and practice history are saved only on your device, never transmitted, and not copied into any cloud backup.

The full privacy policy is short, and says the same thing at greater length.
