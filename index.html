Not transposed and not a capo trick. Inside the app

Five tabs that share one tuning

Metronome, Tuner, Chords, Writer and Voice. The tuning you pick in one is the tuning the others work in.

  • The tuner, mid-pluck on the G string. A dot rides the arc and settles into the green band when you’re in tune. Prefer a straight bar, or both at once? It’s a long-press away. Pick another tuning and the strings, the readout and every chord shape change with it.

  • Explore: a guitar neck you fret with a fingertip. Tap out a shape and Sitka names it (here a G shape in DADGAD with a capo on 2, sounding A), then strums it back to you. Stack a few and send them straight into a song.

  • Every chord in a key as a diagram, worked out for your tuning and capo, with its Roman numeral. Tap one to see every way to play it, ranked.

  • Chords sit above the words where you put them, with a syllable count on every line and sections you can add and reorder. Export as ChordPro or plain text whenever you like.

  • Perform scrolls the song at your tempo, after a count-in, so you can play it through without touching the screen.

  • Set a capo and every shape is worked out from it. Not sure where it goes? Sitka suggests the spots that turn the most chords into open shapes.

Explore

Fret it, and Sitka names it

Put your fingers anywhere on the virtual neck, in any tuning, with or without a capo, and Sitka tells you what chord you’re holding: slash chords and power chords included. It strums the shape back on a plucked-string sound, so you hear it before you play it. Stack a few and drop them into a song, or keep the ones you like in My shapes (with the unlock).

Capo

Play G. Hear B♭.

A capo changes which shapes are easy. Set one and every chord in Sitka is drawn from it: in the chord browser, in your songs and in Perform. Switch between the shape you play and the chord that sounds. And if a song is full of barre chords, Sitka suggests where the capo should go.

What "ranked" means

Three ways to play G. They are not equal.

Every chart gives you a shape. Sitka gives you the ones worth learning first, and says why: open strings ring longer than stopped ones, the root in the bass sounds like the chord you asked for, and fewer fingers means you can change to it in time.

Five points between first and third — one open string, and one finger.

The tuner

Built to be right, not to look busy

Pitch detection on the YIN algorithm, verified against reference tones. It reads a low E as a low E instead of flipping an octave, stays quiet between plucks in a noisy room, and reads the sample rate from your phone's hardware rather than assuming it — the shortcut that leaves some tuners consistently sharp on some phones.

Choose an arc or a straight bar, or both. It ignores mains hum and the tail of a dying note, and if you play the wrong string it tells you which note it heard.

Play the guitar and it will capture the tuning you have already found by ear, string by string (part of the unlock).

Songwriting

Somewhere to put the song

A writing side that knows what key you are in: diatonic chords with roman numerals, borrowed chords when you want the surprise, and a rhyme dictionary that ranks by sound rather than spelling. Chords sit above the words, where you put them.

  • Perform mode with a count-in.
  • A capo per song, with suggestions.
  • Export as ChordPro or plain text, whenever you like.

Also inside

A metronome, and a trainer for your ear

A metronome with meters and subdivisions. And a vocal trainer that reports three numbers rather than one — how long you held the note, whether you sat sharp or flat, and how long you took to find it — because those are three different problems with three different fixes.

It builds every exercise inside the range you gave it and never above it.

Chord practice: progressions like I–V–vi–IV shown one chord at a time with the next one waiting, from open shapes up the neck (the full game is part of the unlock).

Privacy

It stays on your phone

This is not a promise about our intentions. It is a property of how the app is built.

  • Sitka has no servers, no account, no analytics and no ads.
  • The only thing it talks to is Google Play: when the app opens it asks Play whether you own the one-time unlock, it fetches the price when you look at the unlock, and Play shows the occasional ‘rate Sitka’ prompt.
  • Audio is analysed as it arrives and thrown away, never recorded or stored.
  • Your songs, tunings, shapes and practice history stay on your phone, and are excluded from Android’s cloud backup.
  • A backup file is made only when you tap Export, and it goes wherever you send it.

The full privacy policy is short, and says the same thing at greater length.
